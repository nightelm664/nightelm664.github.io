Echo Race to World First campaign

Client / org: Echo Esports Role: Social Media Manager (Part Time) Window: 2025 – Present
25M+views (from CV)
AMDpartner (from CV)
29.1KIG followers @echoguild
119KX followers @EchoGuild

Challenge

Race to World First is one of the most watched competitive moments in World of Warcraft. Echo needed social that could keep pace with a live race: kill announcements, emotional wrap-ups, humour between pulls, partner integrations, and a feed that felt like the guild — not a press release. The audience is deep in the meta; they smell inauthentic content instantly.

Approach

Led the social campaign end-to-end: planning, filming and editing video; working with talent and players for organic and partner deliverables. Partners included AMD (day-in-the-life RWF reel, #EchoAdvancedByAMD). Content mixed real-time hype (world-first kills), end-of-tier storytelling, celebrity and humour moments, and sponsor reels woven into the race narrative rather than bolted on.

Platforms: Instagram @echoguild and X @EchoGuild as the primary org surfaces, with personal channel support where it strengthened reach or culture.

“Not the ending we wanted” — end-of-tier honesty travels further than highlight-reel spin.

Outcome

25M+ views across the campaign (figure from CV — labelled as such). Individual audited posts in the Aug–Sep 2026 window show how the machine performs at moment-level: Twin Fangs world first at 108K views, Venomous Abyss 8/8 wrap at 111K, Moncada visit at 39.7K, Corsair partner reel at 30.5K, plus strong X quote posts (e.g. 92K views).

Campaign total is a CV figure; per-post metrics are public Instagram/X counts from the Oct 2026 work audit.

Why it worked

  • Real-time world-first moments create genuine urgency — the feed becomes the race.
  • End-of-tier emotion and honesty give community payoff when the hype window closes.
  • Partner content (AMD, Corsair) is tied to the narrative, so it reads as part of the story.
  • Humour and celebrity crossovers widen the aperture without abandoning raid culture.
